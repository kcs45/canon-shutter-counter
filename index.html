Establishing PTP Connection

USB Connection Checklist & Troubleshooting

  • 1 Set Camera to PTP Mode: On your Canon, open the menu (Wrench Icon) -> USB Connection Mode, and choose PTP, Photo Transfer, or Remote Control/EOS Utility.
  • 2 Disable Wi-Fi/Bluetooth: Turn off Wi-Fi/NFC/Bluetooth inside your camera's communication settings. (Crucial: Canon automatically disables its physical USB port when wireless is active!)
  • 3 Close Conflicting Apps: Ensure the EOS Utility, Lightroom, Capture One, or other camera-controlling programs are fully closed. They lock the USB interface, preventing browser communication.
  • 4 USB Connection: Turn your camera on, use an appropriate USB cable, and plug it directly into your PC or Mac. Click the Connect button above to select your device in the secure browser prompt.

File Parsing Validation Details:

For modern Canon Mirrorless cameras (such as the R5, R6, R6 II, R8, R50), Exif/CR3 structures store the physical shutter actions in specific MakerNote TIFF containers. For older DSLR cameras (like the 5D Mark IV, 80D, Rebel series), Canon explicitly restricts shutter count from being recorded inside raw image files; for those cameras, the shutter count can only be read via the hardware USB PTP mode. If your camera is an older DSLR, please connect it over USB!

Unable to claim interface

Your browser successfully saw the camera, but the operating system blocked Chrome from opening the device's PTP registers. Let's fix this in 10 seconds:

Windows users (Most Common)

Windows automatically claims the camera to its default MTP driver. To let the browser access it, we need to bind the camera to standard **WinUSB**:

  1. Download the tiny official utility Zadig (https://zadig.akeo.ie/).
  2. Turn your camera on, select **Options** ➔ click **List All Devices** inside Zadig.
  3. Select your Canon camera from the dropdown menu.
  4. Set the target driver to **WinUSB** and click **Replace Driver** (or Reinstall).
  5. Come back here, refresh, and press Connect!
macOS Users

A background system process called `PTPCamera` automatically locks the USB port on launch.

  1. Open your Mac **Terminal** app.
  2. Copy & paste: killall PTPCamera and hit Enter.
  3. Refresh this website page and click Connect!
